Why Byterake

Cleans what's safe, asks about the rest.

No scare warnings, no "PC health score", no upsell. Just where your space went and a careful way to get it back.

Safe by default

Caches and temp files that rebuild themselves are ticked for you. Anything you might miss is marked Review and stays off.

Recycle Bin first

Files go to the Recycle Bin, so you can restore them. Permanent delete is a separate choice that asks twice.

Never-touch list

Windows, Program Files, your Documents, Desktop, Pictures, Videos, Music and .git folders are refused, even if selected.

See where space went

Scan a drive or folder for a sorted tree of the biggest folders and files. Links and junctions are never followed.

Fully offline

No network calls, no AI, no telemetry, no update checks. Every cleanup rule is in a file on your PC.

A log of everything

Every scan, delete and skip is written to a plain-text log, with the reason. Open it from the app.

How it works

Look first, then clean.

Clean in a minute

From the Overview page

  1. Open Byterake. It measures the cleanup catalog straight away: temp files, browser caches, the Recycle Bin and more.
  2. Keep the Safe ticks, or tick Review items you are sure about. The goal ring shows how close you get to your free-space target.
  3. Press Clean selected…. The next screen shows the exact number of files and their size, then you confirm.
  4. Files in use are skipped and listed. When it finishes you can empty the Recycle Bin to get the space back.

Find the big stuff

Scan folders and Review files

  1. Press Scan drive to see every folder sorted by size. A full C: drive usually takes one to three minutes.
  2. On Review files, tick old installers in Downloads or large files over a size you choose. Nothing there is ticked for you.
  3. On Uninstall apps, see programs by size and open a program's own uninstaller.
Safety

What it cleans, and what it never touches.

Cleans (when you tick it)

  • Temp files (yours and Windows')
  • Chrome, Edge and Firefox caches (cache folders only)
  • Thumbnail cache and crash dumps
  • Windows Update and Delivery Optimization caches
  • npm, pip and NuGet caches
  • The Recycle Bin, and files you choose one by one

Never touches

  • System32, WinSxS and Program Files
  • pagefile, hiberfil and swapfile
  • Documents, Desktop, Pictures, Videos, Music
  • Browser profiles, passwords, bookmarks and history
  • .git folders and system files
  • Links and junctions (never followed)

Questions

Why didn't my free space go up?

Files moved to the Recycle Bin still use space until the bin is emptied. When a clean finishes, Byterake offers Empty Recycle Bin now, which asks for its own confirmation.

Why does it ask for admin rights?

Only some items need them: Windows temp, Windows Update downloads, Delivery Optimization and system crash dumps. Byterake tells you which ones first, then restarts as administrator and keeps your ticks. Everything else works with normal rights.

Will it delete my photos or documents?

No. Your Documents, Desktop, Pictures, Videos and Music folders are on a built-in never-touch list that can't be turned off. Large files elsewhere are only listed, and nothing there is ticked unless you tick it.

Does it send anything over the internet?

No. Byterake has no network code at all: no analytics, no update checks, no crash reports. You can read the source on GitHub.

Can I change what it cleans?

Yes. Cleanup targets live in a catalog.json file. Copy catalog.example.json in the install folder to catalog.json and edit it. The never-touch list is built in and can't be changed.

What about Windows.old?

Byterake shows its size and warns that you can't roll back without it, then opens Windows' own Disk Cleanup to remove it, because Windows protects those files.
